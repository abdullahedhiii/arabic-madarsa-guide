Section 1.4

المرکَباتُ وَالجُمَلُ – Sentences and Phrases

مُرَکَبٌ عَدَوُرُ مُفْعِلٌ and مُرَکَبٌ مُفْعِلٌ is of two types:

- كَلَامٌ تَامٌ جُمْلَةٌ مُفْعِلَةٌ، مُرَکَبٌ تَامٌ and مُرَکَبٌ مُفْعِلٌ is also called . This is a complete sentence. Often, it is just called كَلَامٌ .
- كَلَامٌ نَاقِصٌ جُمْلَةٌ عَدَوُرُ مُفْعِلَةٌ، مُرَکَبٌ نَاقِصٌ and مُرَکَبٌ عَدَوُرُ مُفْعِلٌ is also called . This is a phrase, i.e., an incomplete sentence.

From now onwards, when we use “sentence,” we will be referring to complete sentences; and when we use “phrase,” we will be referring to incomplete sentences.

Types of Sentences

There are two types of sentences:

A. جُمْلَةٌ حَرَفِيَّةٌ: It is that sentence, which has the possibility of being true or false.

B. جُمْلَةٌ إِنْشَائِيَّةٌ: It is that sentence, which does not have the possibility of being true or false.

Section 1.4.1

أَقْسَامُ الْجُمْلَةِ اْحْتِرَابِيَّةِ

جُمْلَةٌ إِسْمِيَّةٌ حَرَفِيَّةٌ is of two types:

1. جُمْلَةٌ إِسْمِيَّةٌ حَرَفِيَّةٌ – Nominal Sentence:

Definition: It is that sentence, which begins with a noun.

- The second part of the sentence can be a noun or a verb.
- The first part of the sentence is called مُسْتَدَلٌ إِلَيْهِ (subject) or مُسْتَدَلٌ إِلَيْهِ (the word about which information is being given).
- The second part of the sentence is called خَبَرٌ (predicate) or مُسْتَدَلٌ (the word giving the information).
- The is generally مَعْرُوفَةٌ and the خَبَرٌ generally نَكِرَةٌ.
- Both parts (خَبَرٌ and مُسْتَدَلٌ إِلَيْهِ) are مَرْفُوعَةٌ.
